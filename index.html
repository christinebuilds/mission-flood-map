A volunteer-made map for your city storms. It is not an official city service. Purple is the city's modeled flood zone, dashed areas flooded in past storms per news reports, and dots are recent reports. Tap anything for details. In an emergency call 9-1-1.

Posts are anonymous. Notes and photos are checked automatically before they appear publicly; photos with faces or license plates are not shown. For danger to life call 9-1-1. This does not notify the city; report to 311 for that.

What each layer means

  • City flood zone (purple):
  • Past storms (dashed shading): intersections and streets that news reports say flooded before. These are estimates of where water tends to collect, not a record of this week. Red is a repeat hotspot; orange was reported once or twice.
  • 311 reports (solid dots): flooding-related cases people filed with the city in the window you pick. Blue is flooding, teal is a storm drain needing maintenance, purple is a sewage backup. Pale dots are closed cases.
  • Storm drains: city drain locations from the city's open data. Pink means check now (a neighbor flagged it, or an open 311 flooding or drain case is within 60 m). Amber means check first (inside the city flood zone, or in or next to a past-flood area). Green was cleared by a neighbor in the last 14 days. These rankings are this map's own rule of thumb, not a city assessment. Tap a drain to mark it cleared or flag it, with a photo. To adopt one officially, go to the city's adopt-a-drain program.
  • Neighbor reports (cyan): "water here" posts from people using this map in the last 48 hours. Unverified.
  • Chips above the map: National Weather Service alerts and rain chance, and the next high tide from the nearest NOAA gauge. Heavy rain near high tide drains slowest.
  • Shapes are drawn by hand from the intersections articles name, so edges are accurate to a block or two. News coverage is uneven across neighborhoods, so a street with no shading is not necessarily safe.
