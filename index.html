What the colors mean
  • City flood zone (purple):
  • Past storms (dashed): places news reports say flooded before. Red is a repeat hotspot, orange was reported once or twice. An estimate, not a record of this week.
  • 311 reports (solid dots): blue is flooding, teal a storm drain needing maintenance, purple a sewage backup. Pale means the case is closed.
  • Storm drains: pink means check now (a neighbor flagged it, or an open 311 case is within 60 m). Amber means check first (in the flood zone or a past-flood area). Green was cleared by a neighbor in the last 14 days. This is the map's own rule of thumb, not a city assessment. Tap a drain to mark it cleared or flag it. To adopt one officially: the city's adopt-a-drain program.
  • Neighbor reports (cyan): "water here" posts from the last 48 hours. Unverified.
  • Chips above the map: how much rain is expected (Open-Meteo forecast), weather alerts, rain chance and the next high tide. Heavy rain near high tide drains slowest.
  • Dashed lines follow the city's own street lines for the streets named in news reports. Circles are a rough area around a corner. How far the water spread along each street is an estimate. No shading does not mean a street is safe.
